RESEARCH AREA UUV AUTONOMY CURRENT PHASE ALGORITHM DEVELOPMENT

Peacock Dynamics is an independent robotics research group developing autonomy for underwater vehicles. We build control algorithms and simulation tools for UUVs that need to plan surveys, divide tasks, and coordinate when sensing or communication is limited.

Our current work covers survey planning, task allocation, and vehicle control in simulation. We measure coverage, repeated travel, and vehicle separation. Next, we plan to integrate these methods with vehicle systems and evaluate them in controlled trials. Seabed mapping and environmental monitoring are target applications.

UUV-CONTROL-BENCHMARK Depth and pitch control
UUV-CONTROL-BENCHMARK

Comparing depth and pitch controllers.

We use UUV-Control-Benchmark’s nonlinear depth-and-pitch model to compare LQR, PPO, and residual control under external forces, persistent bias, and model mismatch. This gives us a controlled way to compare responses across those conditions.

MICROTRENCH-2D

Testing local exploration and resource search.

MicroTrench-2D is a CPU-only simulation for testing local search and coverage methods. Each vehicle acts on local observations and records the resources it finds; it cannot access the full world state. Hydrodynamics, vehicle dynamics, and sensor physics are excluded so we can study search behavior separately. Multi-UUV behavior remains future work for this simulator.

Source code Simulator details
MULTIUUV-LINK

Testing cooperation over limited-range links.

MultiUUV-Link is a simulation study with four UUV agents. Each works from local observations and private memory, with messages limited by range. The agents divide exploration frontiers, update routes, predict collisions, and reassign work when one loses contact. In the canonical Hybrid benchmark, mean final coverage was 98.63% and mean redundancy was 0.2337. In a fault-recovery run, the agents found all 10 resources and maintained at least 131.52 px separation against a 128 px minimum. These are simulation results.

RESEARCH BACKGROUND

Earlier robotics work behind our UUV research

10 EARLIER WORKS · 05 RESEARCH AREAS

Our marine research builds on earlier work in aerial and ground robotics, control, perception, mapping, and simulation. These studies and tools are the starting point for our current UUV work.

RESEARCH AREA 01

Local Navigation and Coverage.

MicroUAV-2D let us test local-observation navigation and discrete movement on a CPU-only grid. AgriDroneRL used PPO to plan first-visit coverage across a vegetation utility field. This work gave us tools to test navigation and coverage before adding underwater vehicle physics.

MicroUAV-2D AgriDroneRL
RESEARCH AREA 02

Multi-Robot Coordination.

PPO-Driven Swarm Control combined learned movement, repulsion, graph consensus, and stochastic role assignment. In simulation, it eliminated 138 close encounters while maintaining coordinated coverage. The Boustrophedon Navigator completed lane coverage with a mean cross-track error of 0.0518. We are adapting these methods to marine vehicles, where teams must work with limited communication.

PPO-Driven Swarm Control Boustrophedon Navigator
RESEARCH AREA 03

Guidance and Control Under Disturbance.

InterceptDynamics-Py compared PD guidance with constrained MPC under relative-state dynamics, acceleration limits, and slew-rate limits. In a separate Cart-Pole study, LQR stabilized the system under continuous stochastic disturbance. We draw on this work in our research on UUV trajectory control and disturbance rejection.

Cart-Pole LQR InterceptDynamics-Py
RESEARCH AREA 04

Perception and Mapping.

Reflect-Aug-Seg tested range-aware LiDAR augmentation and found measurable improvement in semantic segmentation using reflectivity proxies. VLMaps stored spatial memory with visual-language embeddings and open-vocabulary queries. Both studies used ground or indoor settings. We are assessing which ideas could transfer to underwater perception and mapping.

Reflect-Aug-Seg VLMaps Reimplementation
RESEARCH AREA 05

3D Mapping and System Integration.

ApolloSplat-Py reconstructed a lunar terrain patch from 15 historical images, producing a 580,552-point cloud and ROS2-ready meshes. TerraDrop-PX4 connected image perception to PX4 control in ROS2 and Gazebo simulation using camera geometry and visual servoing. This work gave us experience with 3D mapping and perception-to-control integration.

ApolloSplat-Py TerraDrop-PX4

RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions shaping our UUV research

  1. 01

    How should a team divide a survey?

    When several vehicles map or inspect an area, how can they divide the work without leaving gaps or repeating each other’s routes?

  2. 02

    How do currents affect vehicle control?

    Currents, drag, sensor noise, and localization drift make a UUV’s motion hard to predict. We study how control methods perform when the model is incomplete.

  3. 03

    What can vehicles do with limited communication?

    Underwater links can be slow or unreliable. We study coordination methods that let each vehicle make local decisions and share updates when a connection is available.

  4. 04

    How do we move from simulation to field tests?

    Our algorithms will need to work with onboard computers, sensors, propulsion, and vehicle software. Integration and controlled trials are the next steps toward evaluating them on marine systems.

We’re building a research program in UUV autonomy and are open to research collaborations, technical partnerships, and investment. Get in touch to discuss integrating and testing these methods with marine vehicles.
